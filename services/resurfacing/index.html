When resurfacing may be appropriate

Persistent staining, a rough or pitted feel, visible wear, peeling areas or a finish that no longer supports the property’s design.

More than a color change

The finish affects how the entire pool looks and feels.

A pool interior is both a protective layer and the visual foundation beneath the water. Its color, texture and condition shape clarity, comfort and the sense of quality around the entire space.

Resurfacing typically involves draining the pool, preparing the existing interior, correcting appropriate surface conditions, applying the selected new finish and completing the startup sequence. The exact scope depends on the current substrate, accessibility, finish choice and any connected tile, fitting or equipment work.

Big Guava approaches the project as a coordinated renewal rather than a single application step. That means considering transitions at the waterline, drain covers, return fittings and the timing required before the pool returns to normal use.

Resurfacing questions

What clients usually want to understand.

Can every crack be solved by resurfacing?

No. Some visible cracks may require a separate repair or further evaluation before resurfacing. The appropriate response depends on the type, location and underlying cause.

Can tile work be coordinated at the same time?

Yes, tile replacement or repair may be coordinated with resurfacing when the project scope calls for it. Sequencing the work together can help create cleaner transitions.

How long will the pool be unavailable?

Timing varies with the finish system, repairs, weather, access and startup requirements. A project-specific schedule should be established after inspection.

Is the pool ready immediately after filling?

New finishes often require a structured startup and water-balancing period. Instructions depend on the selected material and project conditions.
