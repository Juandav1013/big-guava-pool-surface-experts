Focused problem solving

A repair should respond to the actual condition.

A visible defect may be cosmetic, localized or connected to movement, installation history or another pool-system issue. The first step is defining what can be observed and what requires further investigation.

Big Guava’s repair approach centers on the pool surface and its immediate connections. That may include interior finish failures, cracks, damaged areas around fittings, tile transitions or leak-related symptoms visible at the shell.

Not every condition can be confirmed from appearance alone. When specialized leak detection, structural evaluation or additional trade work is appropriate, the scope should be clarified before promising a repair outcome.

Repair questions

Clarity before commitment.

Can a repaired area match the existing finish exactly?

Color and texture matching can be limited by the age, wear and original material of the surrounding surface. The expected visual result should be discussed before work begins.

Does a crack always mean the pool is structurally damaged?

No. Cracks vary widely. Some may be limited to the finish, while others merit deeper evaluation. Classification matters before selecting a repair.

When is resurfacing better than a local repair?

If wear or failure is widespread, the existing finish is near the end of its useful condition, or multiple areas need attention, broader resurfacing may provide a more consistent result.

Can you evaluate equipment at the same visit?

Relevant pumps, filters, heaters or circulation concerns can be discussed so the project scope accounts for the complete pool environment.
